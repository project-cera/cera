# Key Findings: no-SIL Ablation Flagging — Galaxy Book3 (cera-full)

**Date:** March 23, 2026
**Flagger Model:** Claude Opus 4.6 (via Claude Code subagents, 19 parallel batches of 25)
**Domain:** Laptop
**Product:** Samsung Galaxy Book3 (2023)
**Factsheet:** 44 verifiable claims across 14 categories (processor, RAM, storage, display, ports, battery, dimensions, pricing, OS, camera/audio, keyboard, colors, "Does NOT Have")
**Mode:** cera-full (single mode evaluated in this run)

---

| Mode | Total Reviews | Errors (types 1-4) | Error Rate | Vague Reviews | Vague Rate |
|------|--------------|--------------------:|------------|---------------|------------|
| cera-full | 442 | 1 | **0.23%** | 377 | 85.3% |

### Error Rates by Dataset Size

| Size | Reviews | Errors | Error Rate | Vague | Vague Rate |
|------|---------|--------|------------|-------|------------|
| 100-sent | 24 | 0 | 0.00% | 0 | 0.0% |
| 500-sent | 136 | 1 | 0.74% | 120 | 88.2% |
| 1000-sent | 282 | 0 | 0.00% | 257 | 91.1% |

**Note on 100-sent discrepancy:** The 100-sent batch subagent applied a stricter vague threshold, interpreting mentions of "screen," "battery life," and "keyboard" as sufficient product-spec references (per the instruction that "opinions about build quality, price, performance, customer service, screen quality, etc. are NOT vague"). Other batches interpreted the vague criterion more literally, requiring explicit spec numbers (e.g., "16GB RAM," "15.6-inch display"). This inter-agent calibration variance is documented as a limitation.

### The Single Error

**Review #133 (500-sent dataset):** Claims "the screen glare is a nightmare in bright rooms." The factsheet specifies the Galaxy Book3 has an **IPS LCD with matte (anti-glare) finish**. Matte displays are specifically designed to minimize glare. The review's claim of nightmare-level glare implies a glossy panel, contradicting the factsheet.

---

## Dominant Pattern: Vagueness

The overwhelming finding for cera-full mode on this product is **extreme vagueness**. Across all three dataset sizes, the vast majority of generated reviews contain zero references to any specific Galaxy Book3 specification. Reviews consist entirely of generic quality/value/support commentary that could apply to any laptop or consumer electronics product.

Typical vague review patterns observed:
- "The build quality is fantastic and the value for the price is just right"
- "The price point is a total STEAL for the build quality you get"
- "solid performance without breaking the bank"

These reviews reference no processor model, RAM amount, screen size/resolution, port types, battery capacity, weight, or any other verifiable specification from the 44-claim factsheet.

---

## Implications for the SIL Component

This single-mode baseline establishes the cera-full error profile. With only 1 factual error across 442 reviews (0.23% error rate), cera-full with SIL demonstrates strong factual grounding. However, the dominant failure mode is not factual inaccuracy but rather **insufficient specificity** — the generated reviews are too generic to be useful as realistic product reviews.

This suggests that while SIL successfully prevents factual errors (the reviews rarely contradict the factsheet), the generation pipeline may not be leveraging the SIL-gathered intelligence effectively to produce reviews with product-specific detail. The high vague rate (85.3% overall) indicates a generation-side issue rather than a SIL-side issue.

To complete the ablation analysis, cera-no-sil and heuristic modes should be run on the same product to enable comparative error rate analysis and determine whether removing SIL increases factual errors.

---

## Study Limitations

1. **Single mode:** Only cera-full was evaluated in this run; no comparative baseline (cera-no-sil, heuristic) is available yet for this product.
2. **Single domain:** Laptop only. Results may not generalize to restaurant or hotel domains.
3. **Single LLM generator:** Reviews generated by Qwen3.5-35B-A3B. Different LLMs may produce different error profiles.
4. **Flagger model bias:** Claude Opus 4.6 may have different sensitivity thresholds than the web UI flagger or human reviewers.
5. **Inter-agent calibration:** The 19 parallel subagents showed variance in vague-review thresholds (notably the 100-sent batch). Batch-level consistency is not guaranteed.
6. **Vague definition ambiguity:** The boundary between "mentions screen quality (not vague)" and "mentions screen quality but with no spec values (vague)" is inherently subjective.
7. **Product maturity:** The Galaxy Book3 was released in February 2023, over 3 years before the flagging date (March 2026). No timeline errors are possible for any reasonable usage duration claim.
